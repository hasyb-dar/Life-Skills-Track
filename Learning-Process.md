## Learning Process
# ❓ Question 1: What is the Feynman Technique? Explain in 1 line.

The Feynman Technique is a way of learning where you explain an idea in plain, simple language, spot the places where your understanding falls short, and keep refining your explanation until you could teach it to anyone.

# ❓ Question 2: In the Barbara Oakley video, what was the most interesting story or idea for you?

The idea that stood out most to me is that effective learning depends on moving between focused mode and diffuse mode thinking.

Focused mode lets us concentrate on a problem and work through familiar material step by step.

Diffuse mode lets the brain link ideas in the background when we step away or turn our attention elsewhere.

This feels practical to me. When I'm stuck on a programming problem, staring at the same code for longer rarely helps. A short break often lets me return with a clearer head and see the solution I had missed.

# ❓ Question 3: What are active and diffused modes of thinking?
Focused mode: A state in which we concentrate closely on one specific task or problem. For example, writing a Java program, debugging code, or solving a DSA question.

Diffuse mode: A relaxed state in which the brain makes wider connections and processes ideas in the background. For example, going for a walk or taking a short break after struggling with a problem.

# ❓ Question 4: According to the video, what are the steps to take when approaching a new topic? Only mention the points.
Deconstruct the skill, learn enough to self-correct, remove barriers to practice, and practise for at least 20 hours.

# ❓ Question 5: What are some of the actions you can take going forward to improve your learning process?
I will explain concepts in my own words using the Feynman Technique, practise active recall, switch between focused and diffuse thinking with Pomodoro-style study sessions, and avoid distractions.

I will break topics into smaller parts, practise consistently, focus on understanding over memorising, review regularly, and ask for help early after making a genuine effort.
