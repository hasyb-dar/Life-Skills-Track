## Q1: In this video, what was the most interesting story or idea for you?
What stood out to me was Fogg's advice to begin with a **tiny behavior** and link it to something I already do, like 2 push-ups after using the bathroom. Since the task is so small, it takes no willpower, and it gradually grows into a real habit.
## Q2: How can you use B = MAP to make making new habits easier? What are M, A and P.
**Behavior = Motivation + Ability + Prompt** (all three need to meet at the same moment).

* **M (Motivation):** how much you want to do it
* **A (Ability):** how simple it is to do
* **P (Prompt):** the signal that tells you to do it now

To make new habits easier, I can shrink the habit so it is simple to do, tie it to an existing routine as the prompt ("After I brush my teeth, I will floss one tooth"), and not depend on motivation.
## Q3: Why it is important to "Shine" or Celebrate after each successful completion of habit? (This is the most important concept in today's topic. Whatever you celebrate becomes a habit)
Celebrating gives you a **good feeling right after the behavior**, and that feeling is what locks the habit into your brain. Whatever you celebrate becomes a habit, and it also boosts your confidence and keeps you motivated.

## Q5: What is the book's perspective about Identity?
The book says real change starts with changing **who you believe you are**, not only what you want to achieve. Every action is a **vote for the person you want to become** ("I am a person who exercises"), and habits are the proof of that identity.
## Q6: Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit easier to do?
The four laws for building good habits:

* Make it **obvious** (clear cues, habit stacking)
* Make it **attractive** (combine it with something you enjoy)
* Make it **easy** (cut down friction, follow the **2-minute rule**, set up your environment)
* Make it **satisfying** (get an instant reward, track your progress)

## Q7: Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit harder to do?
Flip the four laws:

* Make it **invisible** (take away the cues)
* Make it **unattractive** (think about its downsides)
* Make it **difficult** (add friction, like deleting apps or logging out)
* Make it **unsatisfying** (use an accountability partner, habit contract or penalty)
