# Active Listening and Communication Skills

## 1. What steps or strategies can you follow to become an active listener?

Active listening means giving full attention to the person who is speaking and trying to understand their message.

1. **Give full attention:** Focus on the speaker instead of using my phone or doing other activities.
2. **Avoid interrupting:** Allow the speaker to complete their thoughts before responding.
3. **Maintain eye contact:** Use natural eye contact to show that I am paying attention.
4. **Use positive body language:** Nod, face the speaker, and maintain an attentive posture.
5. **Ask questions:** Ask relevant questions when something is unclear.
6. **Use encouraging words:** Use phrases such as "I understand," "Please continue," or "Can you explain that again?"
7. **Take notes:** Write down important points, especially during meetings or technical discussions.
8. **Paraphrase what I heard:** Repeat the main idea in my own words to check whether I understood it correctly.

## 2. What are the key points of reflective listening according to Fisher's model?

Reflective listening means listening carefully and reflecting the speaker's message back to them to make sure I have understood it correctly.

The key points are:

- Give the speaker my complete attention.
- Try to understand the speaker's point of view instead of immediately judging or giving advice.
- Notice the speaker's emotions, tone of voice, and body language.
- Repeat or summarize the main message in my own words.
- Ask questions to clarify anything I do not understand.
- Give the speaker an opportunity to correct my understanding.
- Respond in a way that shows I have understood both the message and the feelings behind it.

For example, if a teammate explains a problem with an API, I should listen without interrupting, note the important details, and summarize the issue in my own words. I can then ask the teammate whether I understood the problem correctly.

## 3. What are the obstacles in your listening process?

Some obstacles that affect my listening process are:

- **Distractions:** Phone notifications, background noise, or other activities can take my attention away from the speaker.
- **Thinking about my response:** Sometimes I start preparing my answer while the other person is still speaking.
- **Making assumptions:** I may think I already know what the speaker is going to say and stop listening carefully.
- **Impatience:** I may want the speaker to finish quickly instead of giving them enough time.
- **Information overload:** During technical discussions, too much information at once can make it difficult to remember every point.
- **Personal opinions:** Sometimes my own opinions can affect how I understand another person's message.

## 4. How can you improve your listening skills?

I can improve my listening skills by:

1. Keeping my phone away and avoiding other distractions.
2. Giving the speaker enough time to explain their thoughts.
3. Listening to understand instead of listening only to prepare a reply.
4. Avoiding assumptions and keeping an open mind.
5. Asking questions whenever something is unclear.
6. Taking notes during important meetings and technical discussions.
7. Summarizing the speaker's main points to confirm my understanding.
8. Paying attention to the speaker's tone, emotions, and body language.
9. Practising active listening in everyday conversations.

## 5. When do you switch to passive communication?

I may switch to passive communication when I do not want to disagree with someone or when I am worried about hurting their feelings. Sometimes I remain silent even when I have a different opinion.

For example, if my team gives me additional work while I am already busy, I may agree instead of explaining that I have other priorities. I may also avoid raising a concern because I do not want to create a disagreement.

Passive communication can make it difficult for others to understand my needs and may eventually lead to frustration.

## 6. When do you switch to aggressive communication?

I may switch to aggressive communication when I feel very frustrated, angry, or ignored. In such situations, I may speak in a harsh tone, interrupt someone, or try to force my opinion instead of listening to the other person's point of view.

For example, during a team discussion, if my suggestions are repeatedly ignored, I might become impatient and respond rudely. This can hurt the other person and make it harder to solve the actual problem.

I should try to remain calm and express my disagreement respectfully.

## 7. When do you switch to passive-aggressive communication?

I may switch to passive-aggressive communication when I am upset with someone but do not express my feelings directly. Instead, I may show my frustration through sarcasm, indirect comments, cold replies, or silence.

For example, if a teammate does not complete an assigned task, I might make sarcastic comments instead of directly explaining how the delay has affected the team's work.

This style of communication can create confusion and misunderstandings. It is better to explain the problem openly and respectfully.

## 8. How can you make your communication assertive?

Assertive communication means expressing my thoughts, feelings, opinions, and needs clearly and confidently while respecting other people.

I can make my communication more assertive by following these steps:

1. **Understand my feelings:** Identify what I am feeling before responding.
2. **Express my needs clearly:** Explain what I need instead of expecting others to guess.
3. **Use respectful language:** Be direct without insulting, blaming, or threatening anyone.
4. **Learn to say no politely:** If I cannot accept a task, I should explain my situation honestly.
5. **Maintain calm body language:** Use a confident posture, natural eye contact, and a calm tone.
6. **Speak up early:** Discuss problems when they arise instead of keeping them inside until I become frustrated.
7. **Listen to others:** Respect the other person's opinion even when I disagree.
8. **Focus on solutions:** Try to find a solution that considers everyone's needs.
9. **Practise regularly:** Start using assertive communication in simple, everyday situations.

For example, if my manager assigns me a new task while I am already working on an important task, I can say:

"I can take up this task, but I am currently working on another priority. Could you please let me know which task I should complete first?"

This communicates my situation clearly without being rude or refusing unnecessarily.

## Conclusion

Active listening and assertive communication help people understand each other and avoid misunderstandings. By listening carefully, expressing my thoughts clearly, and respecting other people's opinions, I can communicate more effectively in both personal and professional situations.
